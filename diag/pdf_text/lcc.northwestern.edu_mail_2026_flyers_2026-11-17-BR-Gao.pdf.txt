Understanding how cells evolve and change fates in native human 
tissues requires linking their genetic histories to their transcriptional 
states. Long-read single-cell RNA sequencing enables joint profiling of 
expressed genetic variants, gene expression, and transcript isoforms, 
providing a foundation for resolving these relationships. In this 
presentation, I will introduce a high-throughput long-read single-cell 
RNA sequencing approach and computational tools for reconstructing 
clonal evolutionary lineages from somatic variants and integrating 
these lineages with transcriptional profiles. Applications to human 
immune cells and lung tumors reveal the origins and directionality 
of cell-state changes and the coupling between genetic evolution 
and fate remodeling. Together, this work establishes a framework for 
decoding cellular evolution and fate dynamics directly in native tissues 
without experimental lineage labeling, offering new insights into tumor 
progression and adaptation in their physiological and disease contexts.
