Ming Li, PhD, is a Member of the Immunology Program at Memorial 
Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and the David H. Koch Chair in 
Immunologic Studies. His laboratory investigates the tissue-resident 
immune networks that shape organ homeostasis and disease, with a 
particular focus on cancer. Recent research has uncovered how 
tissue-resident innate and adaptive leukocytes mediate cancer 
immunosurveillance, how these barriers are disrupted in primary and 
metastatic tumors, and how they can be therapeutically restored. 
This work is now driving the development of next-generation 
immunotherapies targeting tissue-resident immunity. Dr. Li received 
his PhD in Biological Sciences from Columbia University and 
completed postdoctoral training at Yale University. He is a Fellow of 
the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and has been 
recognized with honors, including a Howard Hughes Medical 
Institute Faculty Scholar Award, a Rita Allen Foundation Scholar 
Award, and the American Association of Immunologists-BD 
Biosciences Investigator Award for outstanding contributions to the 
field of immunology.
